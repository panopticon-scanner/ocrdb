# OCRDb 0.1 — Catalog Audit: Duplicates, Ambiguity, Gaps

**Date:** 2026-08-11
**Status:** External audit of the ratified 0.1.0 taxonomy (381 entries, 7 domains, tag `v0.1.0`). Everything here is a **proposal** — per OCRDb governance, nothing ships unratified. No `domains/` edits were made.
**Question asked:** exploring the catalog as a practicing code reviewer — where are the duplicates, where are the ambiguous boundaries, and what is missing ("when you review code, what do you look for that the catalog can't express")?
**Method:** mechanical sweeps (name-similarity clustering, example-truncation scan, provenance/see_also census) → 6 parallel domain auditors (SEC, COD, ARC, TST, QAL, AGT+DAT) → independent verification of every load-bearing claim by 13 read-only advisors (29 duplicate clusters verdicted; all 45 gap proposals absence-checked) → crosswalk spot-check against public sources. Intermediate artifacts live in `.panopticon/` (untracked; safe to delete after reading).
**Verification ledger:** 29 duplicate clusters verdicted → **19 same-hazard, 9 needs-criteria, 1 rejected**. 45 gap proposals → **45 verified truly-absent** (0 already-covered, 0 catch-all). Crosswalk spot-check (sample, disclosed): CWE-1427, CWE-1336, CWE-1104, OWASP-LLM 2025 LLM05/LLM10 all confirmed real and correctly attached.

---

## Executive summary

The taxonomy's structural engineering is genuinely solid — letter-ordering, reserved-X discipline, empty-category checks, build reproducibility, and the stability contract all verify clean (§5). The problems are exactly the ones the user asked about:

1. **Single-homing is violated in the shipped 0.1.0.** The ratified big-rock-#0 rule ("each hazard lives in exactly one domain") holds for the *named* draft duplicates — but **19 advisor-verified clusters** show the same hazard (often the *same verbatim incident string*) entered under two, sometimes three, codes. Under code-as-identity, each of these is a guaranteed reconcile split for consumers — the exact failure the rule exists to prevent. The failures cluster in three predictable places: the ARC↔QAL border, the ARC-H→TST migration seam (four TST-internal verbatim dups), and migrations that missed a leg (AGT promotion, concurrency re-home).
2. **Boundary documentation is structurally absent.** Zero of 381 entries carry `definition` or `criteria`, so every sibling boundary rides on the kebab name alone — and the auditors filed **69 ambiguity findings** on top of the verified clusters. The 0.1 stability contract froze codes *before* the disambiguation layer existed. `definition`/`criteria` are optional enrichment, so this is fixable without breaking the contract — but it is the single highest-leverage curation investment for 0.2.
3. **The catalog is more complete than expected; the real gaps are specific.** 45 gap proposals were filed from reviewer practice and *all 45 verified absent* — none was already-covered. But adoption collides with the prior-art budget: four domains are already over the ≤25% per-domain ceiling under any reading (§3.4), so gap adoption needs a budget ruling, not just a merge.
4. **Governance drift between docs and data**: `see_also` is adopted in schema/changelog and **100% unpopulated**; the charter says concurrency homes in `COD-G` but it shipped as `COD-F`; the promised SEC→AGT cross-ref stub does not exist; the ratification checklist's truncated-examples fix shipped incomplete; the provenance vocabulary contains annotation garbage and 10 undocumented values.

## 3. Mechanical & governance findings

### 3.1 `see_also` is adopted, promised, and 100% unpopulated
Zero of 381 entries use it. The CHANGELOG credits the 0.1 restructure with "one home each + `see_also` cross-refs"; the RATIFICATION text promises "COD-E1 dissolves to cross-refs", "SEC keeps a cross-reference stub" for AGT (verified: no AGT reference exists anywhere in sec.yml). Consumers get no routing hints for the settled near-miss pairs. Every P0/P1 resolution above names its see_also pairs — populating them is mechanical once rulings land.

### 3.2 Truncated examples shipped in the ratified release
The ratification checklist's mechanical pass item 3 ("truncated `examples` — restore from corpus or trim to clean sentences") shipped incomplete: **36 examples across 26 entries** carry the ~110-character clip signature (SEC 21 entries, AGT 10, QAL 3, ARC 2), e.g. `AGT-C1A` "…forge cross-panel verifi", `SEC-B2C` "…-- the exact an", `AGT-B1A` "…Bash (the exact vector the hook's own project". Full list: `.panopticon/phase0-sweeps.txt`.

### 3.3 Provenance vocabulary is polluted and under-documented
21 distinct values in use; **10 are absent from SCHEMA.md's vocabulary section** (`deep-gap-review` ×44, `gemini-gap-review`, `code-simplifier-agent`, `receiving-code-review-skill`, `openssf-align`, `owasp-align`, …). Five values are annotation garbage that leaked into the list field: `'incl. 1 test-quality-tagged'`, `'incl. 1 correctness-tagged'`, `'1 correctness-tagged'`, `'correctness-tagged'`, `'incl. 1 test-coverage-tagged'` (all QAL). Two entries use bare `corpus` where `corpus-4x` is meant (`COD-F1C`, `DAT-C1B`). `validate.py` checks only that provenance is a non-empty list — it cannot catch any of this. Fix: controlled vocabulary in the validator + a SCHEMA.md vocabulary update.

### 3.4 The prior-art budget is ambiguous — and already exceeded per-domain
SCHEMA.md caps prior-art at "≤ 25% **of entries per domain**"; the CHANGELOG defends 0.1 with the **catalog-wide** share (~19%). Counting prior-art-only entries (no corpus tag) per domain in shipped 0.1.0: **DAT 95%, AGT 44%, COD 34%, SEC 26%** — four domains over the per-domain ceiling (ARC 0%, TST 6%, QAL 5%). Either the ceiling is catalog-wide (fix SCHEMA.md) or per-domain (four domains are over, and DAT/AGT/COD gap adoption below needs corpus grounding or a budget ruling). This directly gates §4.

### 3.5 Doc drift
- `CHARTER.md:33` says "concurrency in `COD-G`" — shipped cod.yml has concurrency at **COD-F**; COD-G is data-representation-and-time. (RATIFICATION/consumer-mapping text says COD-G throughout — draft numbering that survived the re-letter pass.)
- `CHANGELOG.md` claims the restructure applied `see_also` cross-refs — it did not (§3.1).
- RATIFICATION's post-ratification Gate-A domain-unpinned re-run is still outstanding (recorded as deferred — kept here as a process note, and §1 gives it new motivation).

## 6. "When I review code, what do I look for?" — the coverage answer

The six auditors' full checklists are in Appendix D (99 rows). The synthesized picture:

- **Well covered** (found immediately, correctly homed): the injection family, secrets, supply-chain vulns, concurrency/TOCTOU, time/numeric representation, error-swallowing, test quality/flakiness, doc drift, duplication, dead code, naming, complexity, CI architecture, observability basics.
- **Covered but under-scented**: web XSS (SEC-B1C's examples are report-generation-only — web reviewers may not find it); password-KDF weakness (resolves via SEC-C3B criteria, not a new entry); resilience/timeout/retry (deliberately parked in incubating OPS — correct, but OPS is the domain reviewers will reach for first in service code).
- **Genuinely missing** (the §4 shortlist): object-level authorization, missing authentication, transport/storage cleartext, SSTI, file upload, mass assignment; non-file resource leaks, mutable defaults, swapped arguments; regression-test discipline, coverage-gaming; TODO/debug/wildcard-import hygiene; contract evolution/deprecation windows; outbox/cascade-delete data patterns; the four AGT classes.
- **Deliberately declined, correctly**: generic input-sanitization catch-all (would defeat X0X), OPS/A11Y/I18N content pending shaped mining runs.

One scope note from the SEC auditor worth a curation decision: **SEC-G2A is written in panopticon panel/lens vocabulary** ("scout/lens/panel" surface names) — the one SEC-G entry that doesn't generalize to non-scanner projects; a tool-neutral rename is free *now* (pre-1.0 renames are cheap; post-1.0 they cost a deprecation cycle). Same logic applies to any entry whose *name* (not just examples) embeds panopticon vocabulary.

## 8. Appendix E — tooling nits (from audit orientation; secondary to content)

- **Grammar vs declared incubating domains**: the code regex `^[A-Z]{3}-[A-Z][1-9][A-Z]$` cannot express two of the three declared incubating domains — `A11Y` and `I18N` are 4 characters with digits. Decide before 0.2: rename the domains, or widen the grammar (a grammar change is a schema-version event).
- `tools/build_catalog.py`: interpolates the entries JSON into `<script>` without escaping `</` — a `</script>` in free-text `examples` breaks the page (injectable markup in a "self-contained" artifact); also embeds `examples` in the page's DATA blob that the JS never renders (~40% dead payload).
- `tools/validate.py` blind spots: no provenance-vocabulary check (§3.3), no `see_also` target check, no `automated_by` format check, no duplicate-domain-file check; docstring still describes single-homing warnings as "pre-ratification".
- `tests/test_tools.py`: unclosed-file `ResourceWarning`s (ironic, given TST-D1B), stale comment referencing `QAL-B2B` while asserting `QAL-B2A`, hardcoded 381-count coupling (acceptable as a release guard, but expect to bump each release).
- `.github/workflows/validate.yml`: no `--baseline` stability check in CI (the contract is only enforced when someone passes the flag — the release process should wire the previous bundle in).
